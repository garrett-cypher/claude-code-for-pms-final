Rook Dispatch · Release 4.2 · Final report

Four responders went silent, and the system made sure they stayed that way.

Release 4.2 shipped on 12 August with two changes bundled together. One of them, a shorter ping wait, turned slow answers into misses. A scoring rule that never forgives then turned those misses into silence. This report shows how I found that, the fix I prototyped, and the review habit I saved so the gaps behind it get caught earlier.

The chain that turned a shorter timer into silence

Trigger (likely)Ping wait cut from 90s to 60s. Answers at 60–90s now count as missed.
→
PenaltyA miss costs −0.12, the same as a turn-down. history.py
→
No recoveryOnly a "yes" adds +0.08. Nothing decays or resets (2019 TODO).
→
Rank fallsLow score, lower in the ask order, fewer pings.
→
SilenceFewer pings means fewer chances to earn the score back.

Confidence: I put the 60s cut at about 65–75% likely to explain the jump in misses, and 40–50% likely to be the whole root cause. This is a judgement, not a measurement. Ravi's seconds-to-answer data would settle it.

X-Ray Vision · the code

The score only goes down on a miss, and only a "yes" brings it back

Prompt 8Find me the part of this code that takes points off somebody when they miss a ping or turn one down. Show it to me and explain it in plain English. Then find me every single thing in this code that puts points back on.
Findingrecord_declined in history.py subtracts 0.12 for a miss and for a turn-down alike. record_accepted adds 0.08 and only on a yes. Nothing recovers a score over time or resets it. A quiet responder only gets work back by being offered something the higher-ranked people pass on, then answering inside 60 seconds.

3 · The fix, through the prototype

A way back for responders who have gone quiet

The brief (05-super-speed/brief.md) fixes the trap, not the trigger. A miss counts for less than a turn-down, the penalty eases over time, and the handler's console says plainly who has gone quiet and why.

For the quiet responder

A run of misses no longer ends in silence. They drift back into the ask order and get pinged again without anyone having to ask first.

For handlers like Kip and Aunt Dot

The console flags who has gone quiet, with a reason such as "pings down from about 13 a week to 3; 5 of the last 6 missed, none turned down".

Clickable mock, open it full screen. "Quiet" in the mock is my own rule: pings down 50% or more and 40% or more missed since 12 Aug. It flags Vesper, The Undertow, Meteor Mite and Farlight. The thresholds need Ravi's data.

Deliberately not doing

A revert of 4.2, guaranteed pings, a handler setting, or anything that links a cover identity to a legal one (Security Policy 4.1).

Will not fix on its own

The jump in tickets. The 60s ping wait is the likely trigger and is a separate decision for Helen and Marcus.

4 · Keeping it from happening again

The review-checklist skill

4.2 went out with a timer change nobody can explain, a Q3 roadmap nobody owned any more, and a "Committed" item that never made the release notes. The skill makes the four questions I'd otherwise forget asking automatic, for any brief.

CheckQuestion4.2 gap it targets
1 · OwnerIs a person or role named?Q3 items all owned by Priya, who has left
2 · Success measureHow will we know it worked?No baseline for "ping timeout tuning", so nobody noticed the cost
3 · Scope, start vs endDoes the end match the start?Two changes shipped together, effects confounded
4 · Problem before fixIs the problem evidenced first?A 60s cut with no recorded problem it solved

On my own brief

Prompt 2 was run /review-checklist. Verdict: Fix first I then added an owner (my own assumption), a success-measures table and a "what this will not fix" note. The targets in that table are proposals, not agreed with Helen or Ravi.

On the four existing briefs

A scheduled run on 13 Oct (scheduled-run-output.txt) flagged all four, one flag each:

Bulk Calloutno owner

What it does not do: it reviews briefs, not code. It would not have stopped the config change on its own, only asked the questions about it that were never asked. Prompt 4 ("execute this brief") was read as fixing the brief, not building it, because nothing should be built until the open items below are answered.
